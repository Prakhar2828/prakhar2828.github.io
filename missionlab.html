01 / MY CONTRIBUTION

Why I made it.

What I was trying to understand

I wanted to be able to pause the flight at a maneuver and ask where Orion was, what NASA reported, and what I could calculate from the trajectory. For atmospheric entry, that meant reconstructing the geometry, then using a model to estimate external heating. I put the sources and assumptions beside the results so someone else could follow the same reasoning or question it.

My contribution

I built the public-data ingestion and normalization pipeline, deterministic Python/SPICE analysis, bounded engineering models, interactive experiences, evidence architecture and publication layer. NASA/ESA data, imagery and source material retain their external attribution.

03 / THE REASONING BEHIND THE BUILD

What I had to think through.

  1. Preserve the precision of the source.

    A NASA-reported event with minute-level precision stays a minute-level anchor. A derived geometry result can carry a more precise calculation timestamp. Neither is promoted into an assertion about Orion’s onboard navigation solution.

  2. Make “unknown” an acceptable answer.

    The evidence-grounded analyst uses deterministic retrieval and calculations without requiring a paid model or API key. Optional language-model presentation cannot replace that evidence layer or invent a missing targeting vector, telemetry value or root cause.

  3. Put the model boundary next to the interaction.

    Trajectory what-ifs distinguish public scalar burn magnitudes from modeled directions. Entry geometry is reconstructed; external heating is modeled. Space-weather flux is not silently converted into astronaut dose.
